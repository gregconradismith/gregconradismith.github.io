Follow timing into a population signal

Each dot is a simplified phase oscillator representing a neuron’s rhythmic dendritic current source. The hand and wheel show its position within a cycle. A raster mark records an upward crossing of a full cycle; it is an illustrative timing event, rather than a simulated action-potential waveform. Both groups contain the same distribution of natural frequencies.

  • Independent neurons: start with scattered phases and zero coupling. Raise coupling to see a collective rhythm emerge, or use Common input and watch temporary alignment disperse.
  • Lock together: coupling attracts phases toward one common phase. Increase frequency spread or noise, or lower coupling, to weaken coordination.
  • Two alternating groups: second-harmonic coupling supports two phases half a cycle apart. The balanced preset starts with matched pairs in opposite phases and no noise. R₁ can be near zero while R₂ is high. Smooth, equally oriented group contributions cancel in this idealized example. Reverse group B dipoles to make the same activity reinforce at the electrode. With equally oriented dipoles, adding the second harmonic to the current waveform leaves a rhythm near twice the cycle frequency.

Timing, currents, and EEG

The EEG-like trace is the average of signed current dipole contributions. Real scalp EEG arises from neuronal transmembrane currents, their spatial arrangement, and electrical conduction through the head. Here, every source has the same simplified electrode weight except for the optional reversal of group B. The app illustrates addition and cancellation; it does not compute a realistic head model or calibrated microvolts. The natural rhythm is adjustable in hertz without assigning a specific brain circuit or behavioral state to each frequency.

The assigned dipole waveform is pᵢ = cos θᵢ + a cos(2θᵢ), with a = 0 or 0.35. The field is y = (1/N) Σ wᵢpᵢ. Weights are +1 for group A and +1 or −1 for group B. The blue and orange group contributions are each divided by the total N, so their sum is the dark trace. Dividing by N keeps the comparison focused on coordination as the neuron count changes. Six faint individual traces show the unweighted current waveform of three sources from each group.

Changing dipole orientation or waveform immediately recalculates the retained field history from the same neuronal phases. This lets you compare electrode signals while paused, with the raster and dynamics unchanged. Other live parameters affect subsequent dynamics; the time window and spectrum can include both the earlier and later regimes.

Phase dynamics and measurements

The stochastic phase equation is dθᵢ = [2πfᵢ + K Rₕ sin(ψₕ − hθᵢ)]dt + √(2D)dWᵢ, where Rₕ exp(iψₕ) = (1/N) Σ exp(ihθⱼ). Ordinary alignment uses h = 1; the alternating preset uses h = 2. This second-harmonic interaction is a mathematical clustering mechanism, rather than a model of an inhibitory synapse. Noise increments are independent across neurons.

R₁ measures concentration around one phase. R₂ measures concentration modulo half a cycle; both a single cluster and two opposite clusters can have high R₂. Read both measures and the phase wheel together. Common input is an idealized instantaneous phase reset, not a modeled sensory pathway or synaptic stimulus. Scatter phases changes timing without changing the natural-frequency distribution. Both actions retain time and prior history.

Integration uses a fixed 0.002 s step, independent of playback speed, with signal samples every 0.004 s. Up to 12 s of history are retained; plots display the last 2 or 6 s. The Hann-windowed, mean-subtracted spectrum uses up to 1024 samples (4.096 s; 0.244 Hz bin spacing) and a fixed power scale. It shows one-sided power spectral density in a.u.²/Hz. Field RMS and mean phase-crossing rate use the last 2 s. The rate can vary during transients even when the natural-frequency mean is unchanged.

Background: Hansel, Mato & Meunier (1993), Clustering and slow switching in globally coupled phase oscillators; Hagen et al. (2018), Multimodal modeling of neural network activity with LFPy 2.0. Compare individual-neuron dynamics in the FitzHugh–Nagumo network simulator.
