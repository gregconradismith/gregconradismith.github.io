Model and classroom comparisons

Try moving the probe twice as far from the center. Compare the amount of peak voltage lost and the change in peak timing. With the probe fixed, switch between EPSP and IPSP at the same conductance, then compare a brief current pulse with a long one.

The uniform cable has sealed ends, so axial current cannot leave through either end. Voltage changes spread by axial current and decay through the membrane leak. There are no voltage-gated channels. The time shift between peaks describes passive filtering.

The resting potential is −65 mV. Synaptic conductance has the same time course in both modes (0.8 ms rise constant and 5 ms decay constant). The synaptic current is gsyn(Esyn − V). The reversal potentials are 0 mV for the EPSP and −80 mV for the hyperpolarizing IPSP in this example.

Cm is 1 µF/cm² and axial resistivity is 100 Ω·cm. The finite-volume cable solver uses backward Euler. The dashed curve shows the theoretical steady-state voltage ratio for a maintained center current in this finite cable, cosh((L/2 − x)/λ) / cosh(L/(2λ)), where x is distance from the center.
